# Reference solution — S29

## Before you compare

Finish the mission first. The reference below is a **defensible answer pattern**, not the only acceptable implementation.

## Reference diagnosis / solution

Turn the reliability target into an error budget and connect incidents to budget burn. The decision should specify what evidence would pause changes or trigger recovery rather than treating the SLO as a dashboard number.

## What the mission was asking you to prove

Checkout support depends on a service that occasionally fails. Product wants fewer visible failures; engineering wants room to release changes. The disagreement is about the reliability target, not merely the latest incident. Translate the user-facing expectation into an SLO, calculate the error budget, reconstruct recent consumption, and recommend an operating policy for releases and recovery.

## Compare your evidence

A strong submission should contain:

- the business symptom and impact;
- at least two plausible hypotheses and the evidence that separated them;
- the smallest justified implementation/configuration change;
- a regression or acceptance test;
- before/after measurements where applicable;
- a short technical explanation and a short business explanation;
- the operational implication: monitoring, prevention, recovery, security or cost as relevant;
- if an agent was used, the work contract, permissions, independent verification, challenge attempted and human decision.

## Definition of done

1. Define the service-level indicator and SLO in user-visible terms. 2. Calculate the corresponding error budget. 3. Attribute recent failures consistently. 4. Show what the budget implies for release velocity. 5. Define an escalation threshold and recovery expectation. 6. Explain the business consequence of both under- and over-targeting reliability.

## If your answer differs

That is acceptable when your evidence supports it and your solution respects the ticket constraints. Compare the **reasoning and evidence**, not just the final code or wording.
